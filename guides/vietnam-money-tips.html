Vietnam money tips: banknotes, ATMs, cards and common tricks

Updated September 2026

1. Reading prices

  • “50k” on a menu means 50,000 ₫.
  • Vietnam uses a dot as the thousands separator: 50.000 is fifty thousand, not fifty.
  • Coins are almost never used. The smallest note you’ll normally see is 1,000 ₫.

Keep big notes separate from small ones in your wallet. When you pay with a large note, say the amount out loud (“five hundred”) and count your change before walking away.

3. ATMs

  • Most ATMs charge a fee per withdrawal, shown on screen before you confirm, and many cap a single withdrawal at a few million dong. Your home bank may add its own fee, so fewer, larger withdrawals are cheaper.
  • If the ATM or card machine offers to charge you in your home currency, choose VND. The “convenience” rate is usually worse.
  • Use ATMs inside banks or malls when you can.

4. Card or cash?

Hotels, malls, supermarkets, chain cafés and many restaurants take Visa and Mastercard. Street food, markets, small shops and motorbike taxis are cash. A multi-currency card that charges the mid-market rate saves a few percent on every payment.

5. Exchanging money

Change only a small amount at the airport. Rates in the city are usually better. Bring clean, undamaged notes; torn or marked foreign notes may be refused.

6. Avoiding common tricks

  • Note swap: you hand over 500,000, and the seller claims it was 20,000. Say the amount as you pay.
  • Unclear price: agree the price before a cyclo, xe ôm (motorbike taxi) or massage. Grab shows the fare before you book.
  • “Per person” or “per 100 g”: at seafood places, confirm how the price is counted before they cook.

7. Tipping

Not expected in local eateries. At spas, on tours and for good hotel service, 20,000–50,000 ₫ (or around 5–10%) is appreciated. Check the bill for a service charge first.
